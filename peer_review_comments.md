Referee

Thank you for submitting your manuscript . I have now received assessments from two independent reviewers and have completed my own editorial evaluation. Both reviewers recognize the timeliness and conceptual interest of your work, and I share that appreciation — the use of Starship's payload capacity in the context of Europa exploration is a legitimate and underexplored topic.

However, both reviewers have identified concerns serious enough to require a Major Revision before the manuscript can be considered further. I would like to draw your attention to what I consider the most critical issues, which cut across both reviews:

First and foremost, the architectural assumptions underlying the mission concept must be made fully explicit and critically assessed. A refueling architecture requiring 37 on-orbit operations is a central — and currently unproven — premise of the entire analysis. This should be acknowledged as such throughout the paper, including in the abstract, so that the performance claims are correctly interpreted as conditional on that architecture rather than as reflecting inherent vehicle capability.

Second, the mass budget requires a more rigorous treatment. Mission-specific mass costs — radiation shielding, extended communications, Europa landing hardware, cryogenic management systems — appear to have been omitted, and no design margins have been applied. Similarly, the scientific justification for a 10-tonne surface payload should be made explicit: what scientific objectives would motivate and justify this capability, and how does it compare to prior and planned missions?

Third, the feasibility of multi-year cryogenic propellant storage must be addressed with substantially more rigor. As propellant retention is mission-critical for Jupiter orbit insertion and Europa landing, the current steady-state thermal estimate is insufficient justification.

Beyond these technical points, the manuscript requires significant improvements in focus, structure, source quality, and formatting. The use of social media posts as scientific sources is not acceptable. Figures require proper captions, legends, and contextual descriptions. Physical quantities should be expressed consistently in SI units. The paper would benefit considerably from a tighter focus on the trajectory and mission analysis, with less breadth and more analytical depth.

The manuscript under review addresses the feasibility of utilizing SpaceX’s Starship for transporting scientific payloads to Jupiter’s moon Europa. The paper outlines several critical aspects of such a mission, including the potential for significantly increasing payload capacity compared to previous mission concepts, the development of efficient mission plans and trajectories to optimize the delta-V budget and minimize travel time, as well as considerations of radiation effects and cryogenic fuel boiloff.

The topic is timely and innovative, addressing the interface between advanced spacecraft capabilities and ambitious scientific exploration. The use of a commercial spacecraft with unprecedented payload capacity offers promising opportunities for the advancement of the exploration of Europa, and the authors deserve credit for exploring this novel concept.
In conclusion, while the manuscript presents an innovative and promising concept, I suggest to reject the paper, especially due to the lack of focus which in consequence leads to fail the standards of publication in a scientific journal.
